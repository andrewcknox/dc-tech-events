### Sam Gilliam Lecture Series: Jeffrey Gibson
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/12/sam-gilliam-lecture-series-jeffrey-gibson/
Published: 
When: November 12th 5:00pm - 7:00pm
Where: Hopkins Bloomberg Center
Join the Hopkins Bloomberg Center for the next installment of the Sam Gilliam Lecture Series featuring Jeffrey Gibson.
A multidisciplinary Indigenous American artist who is a member of the Mississippi Band of Choctaw and is of Cherokee heritage, 
Gibson
 is renowned for blending Indigenous and Western traditions across painting, installation, video, and performance. In 2024, he became the first Indigenous artist to represent the United States with a solo presentation at the U.S. Pavilion at the Venice Biennale. Through his vibrant practice, Gibson explores identity, community, and the vitality of Indigenous cultures.
A fireside chat following the Sam Gilliam Lecture will be moderated by 
Melissa Walls
, a scholar of Couchiching First Nation and Bois Forte Band of Ojibwe heritage, a Bloomberg Associate Professor of American Health, and the co-director of the Center for Indigenous Health at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health.
About the Sam Gilliam Lecture Series
The Sam Gilliam Lecture Series welcomes prominent artists and speakers to the Johns Hopkins University Bloomberg Center to reflect on the intersections between contemporary art, academia, and public policy, and the role art plays in advancing society.

### Stories That Matter Presents "The Cycle of Love"
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/30/stories-that-matter-film-screening-the-cycle-of-love/
Published: 
When: November 30th 6:00pm - 8:30pm
Where: Hopkins Bloomberg Center
Stories that Matter presents a special screening of 
The Cycle of Love
, an inspiring, heartwarming and epic true-life adventure directed by Oscar-winning filmmaker Orlando von Einsiedel (
The White Helmets
). In the film, a young Indian street artist embarks on an extraordinary 6,000-mile bicycle journey from India to Sweden in 1977 to demonstrate his love and devotion. The film explores the depth of love, the courage to overcome social and cultural barriers, and what it means to risk everything for what your heart tells you.
The screening will be followed by a panel discussion featuring director von Einsiedel and Anand Pandian, the Krieger-Eisenhower Professor of Anthropology and Director of Graduate Studies at the Johns Hopkins Krieger School of Arts and Sciences. The discussion will be moderated by Sig Libowitz, director of the Johns Hopkins Graduate Film and Media Program.
