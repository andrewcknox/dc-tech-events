### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### Civil and Systems Engineering Seminar Series: Large-Scale Physical Simulations of Complex Dynamic Systems
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/19/civil-and-systems-engineering-seminar-series-large-scale-physical-simulations-of-complex-dynamic-systems/
Published: 
When: November 19th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Where: Maryland Hall, Homewood Campus
Barbara Simpson <https://engineering.stanford.edu/people/barbara-simpson>
, assistant professor of civil and environmental engineering at Stanford University, will give a talk titled "Large-Scale Physical Simulations of Complex Dynamic Systems" as part of the 
Department of Civil and Systems Engineering <https://engineering.jhu.edu/case/>
's Fall 2026 Seminar Series.
Abstract:
Large-scale experimental testing can holistically represent the dynamic and nonlinear response of complex civil systems. Beyond the generation of unique datasets, these experiments provide opportunities to demonstrate proof-of-concepts and translate established structural engineering methods across application domains. This presentation describes two complementary areas of large-scale testing: the seismic performance of building structures and the dynamic response of marine energy systems. Representative testing programs include: (i) full-scale shake-table testing of high-performance mass-timber and steel seismic-force-resisting systems; and (ii) real-time hybrid simulation of offshore marine and wind-energy systems. These testing programs were conducted at the NHERI Experimental Facility Large High-Performance Outdoor Shake-Table at the University of California, San Diego; E-Defense in Japan; and the NHERI Experimental Facility O.H. Hinsdale Wave Research Laboratory at Oregon State University. While the shake-table experiments advance understanding of resilient, high-performance building systems, the marine energy experiments investigate structural response in offshore environments, where multi-hazard and multi-directional loading, fluid-structure interaction, and coupled dynamic response introduce new challenges. Collectively, these studies demonstrate how large-scale dynamic testing can provide critical data for civil infrastructure with limited experimental evidence and poorly characterized nonlinear response. This work highlights the expanding role of structural engineering in advancing the design, reliability, and resilience of increasingly diverse infrastructure applications.
Biography:
Barbara Simpson is currently an assistant professor at Stanford University. She uses advanced computational and experimental methods to characterize structural response. Her aim is to develop innovative structural systems that improve building performance and reduce the effects of natural hazards on the built environment. Simpson's research areas include resilient and sustainable design and retrofit of building structures and offshore renewable energy systems, performance-based earthquake engineering, and next-generation computational modeling. One of her current pursuits is the development of real-time hybrid simulation frameworks for fluid-structure interaction problems. Simpson received her doctorate from the University of California, Berkeley, and her Bachelor of Science from the University of Kansas.

### Annual Bell Lecture in Continuum Mechanics
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/19/annual-bell-lecture-in-continuum-mechanics/
Published: 
When: November 19th 3:00pm - 4:00pm
Where: Shaffer Hall, Homewood Campus
Irene J. Beyerlein <https://materials.ucsb.edu/people/faculty/irene-j-beyerlein>
, a distinguished professor with a joint appointment in the Mechanical Engineering and Materials Departments at the University of California at Santa Barbara, will give the Annual Bell Lecture in Continuum Mechanics titled "Interesting Behavior of Dislocations in Refractory Metals and Alloys at High Temperature" for the 
Department of Mechanical Engineering <https://me.jhu.edu/>
.
A reception will follow in the Bloomberg Student Center, room 210 (a Johns Hopkins ID may be required for entry).
Abstract:
Performance demands on structural materials continue to rise, driven by the need for greater energy efficiency, higher operating temperatures, and longer lifetimes. While the development of conventional alloys has unfolded over the years, modern technological challenges are advancing far more rapidly. Refractory body-centered cubic metals, alloys, and more recently, refractory high entropy alloys, exhibit a complex, and often favorable, mechanical response to temperature. Understanding the thermomechanical behavior of this material class benefits from knowledge of dislocation dynamics, including glide mechanisms, critical stresses, and mobility. The formulation and findings from a mesoscale model called phase field dislocation dynamics, built for capturing the favorable pathways of discrete dislocations in deformed refractory body-centered cubic materials at finite temperature will be presented. We will focus on transitions in glide mechanisms resulting from changes in temperature, alloying, and interstitials. The phenomena discussed will be athermal strength, the ductile-brittle transition, solute pinning and drag, and Portevin–Le Chatelier effect.
About the speaker:
In addition to her appointments in the Mechanical Engineering and Materials Departments at the University of California at Santa Barbara, Irene J. Beyerlein is also the Robert and Victoria Mehrabian Interdisciplinary Professor. After receiving her doctorate in theoretical and applied mechanics at Cornell University in 1997, she began a postdoctoral appointment as a J.R. Oppenheimer Fellow at the Los Alamos National Laboratory, where she remained on the scientific staff in the theoretical division until 2016. She is an editor for 
Acta Materialia
 and 
Scripta Materialia
. She is a member of the National Academy of Engineers and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. Over the last decade, she has been awarded the Los Alamos National Laboratory Fellow's Prize for Research (2012), the International Plasticity Young Researcher Award (2013), The Minerals, Metals & Materials Society (TMS) Distinguished Scientist/Engineering Award (2018), the Brimacombe Metal (2019), Materials Research Society Fellowship (2021), TMS Fellowship (2023), and Magnesium Person of the Year (2023).

### Lunch with the Libraries &amp; Museums: Adopt-a-Resource
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/20/lunch-with-the-libraries-museums-adopt-a-resource/
Published: 
When: November 20th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
From medieval manuscripts and rare books to films, photographs, and archival materials, the Sheridan Libraries acquire and preserve important collections that fuel research, teaching, and discovery at Johns Hopkins and beyond. Through the Adopt-a-Resource program, donors help protect and share these treasures by supporting their acquisition, conservation, and digitization.

, accessioning archivist, Special Collections, University Archives

Presented in partnership with

 and
