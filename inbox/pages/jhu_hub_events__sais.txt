### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### LCSR Seminar Series: Adaptive Control and Automated Design for Aerial Robotic Challenges
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/18/lcsr-seminar-series-mark-mueller-adaptive-control-and-automated-design-for-aerial-robotic-challenges/
Published: 
When: November 18th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Where: Hackerman Hall, Homewood Campus
Mark W. Mueller <https://me.berkeley.edu/people/mark-w-mueller/>
, an associate professor of mechanical engineering at the University of California, Berkeley, will give a talk titled "Adaptive Control and Automated Design for Aerial Robotic Challenges" for the 
Laboratory for Computational Sensing + Robotics <https://lcsr.jhu.edu/>
.
Abstract:
Aerial robotics have become ubiquitous, but (like most robots) they still struggle to operate at high speed in unstructured, cramped environments. In this talk I will present two ideas related to creating more capable aerial systems. First, I will present an approach to simultaneously design control and mechanical properties of a system, specifically considering a system for carrying a load with multiple aerial robots. A conventional design approach typically designs the mechanical aspects of the system for open loop characteristics (such as thrust-to-weight ratio) first, and then afterward designs the feedback controller for it. Instead, we present an approach that uses insights from robust control to design both simultaneously, relying on modeling system dynamics and external disturbances. The second part of the seminar will discuss a learning approach for creating robust and adaptable control strategies for quadcopters. We demonstrate a control strategy able to search over very large parametric uncertainty (in experiment, spanning two orders of magnitude in inertia), and able to almost instantaneously respond to changes in parameters (like offsets in center of mass, or reduction in effectiveness of a motor).
About the speaker:
Mueller started at Berkeley in 2016, after a brief stint deploying drones in a play in the biggest theater on New York's Broadway. He received his bachelor's degree from the University of Pretoria in South Africa and master's and doctoral degrees from ETH Zurich in Switzerland. He leads the HiPeRLab (High Performance Robotics Lab), which focuses on design, analysis, and control for autonomous flight.

### DMSE Fall 2026 Seminar Series: Exploiting Molecular Materials for Quantum Information Science
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/18/dmse-fall-2026-seminar-series-exploiting-molecular-materials-for-quantum-information-science/
Published: 
When: November 18th 3:00pm - 4:00pm
Where: Maryland Hall, Homewood Campus
Michael R. Wasielewski <https://chemistry.northwestern.edu/people/faculty/profiles/michael-wasielewski.html>
, a professor of chemistry and applied physics at Northwestern University, will give a talk titled "Exploiting Molecular Materials for Quantum Information Science" for the 
Department of Materials Science & Engineering <https://engineering.jhu.edu/materials/>
.
Abstract
Molecular electron spins have emerged as versatile quantum bits (qubits) with wide ranging applications in quantum sensing, communications, and computing at the nanoscale. These applications take advantage of the ability of chemical synthesis to 1) create specific structures with atomic scale spatial control; 2) tailor the magnetic, optical, and electronic properties of the qubits; and 3) construct large-scale ordered arrays of qubits via crystal growth and self-assembly. In order to take further advantage of the tunability of molecular qubits, optical-spin interfaces must be developed to permit addressing and readout of spin information. We will describe recent work from our laboratory that focuses on using photo-driven processes to generate optical-spin interfaces in molecular materials targeting quantum information applications. We will show how ultrafast photogeneration of electron-hole pairs within covalent electron donor-acceptor systems results in two entangled spins having sufficiently long coherence times to perform a variety of operations relevant to quantum information. We will also describe how molecular chirality can be used to control the spin dynamics of photogenerated electron-hole pairs in donor-acceptor molecules. Finally, we will show how molecular diradicals with triplet ground states can serve as versatile quantum sensors using optical spin polarization, microwave manipulation, and optical readout.
Bio
In addition to his position at Northwestern University, Michael Wasielewski is director of the Institute for Quantum Information Research and Engineering (INQUIRE) and director of the Center for Molecular Quantum Transduction (CMQT), an Energy Frontier Research Center at Northwestern affiliated with the U.S. Department of Energy. He received his Bachelor of Science, Master of Science, and doctorate degrees from the University of Chicago and was a postdoctoral fellow at Columbia University. His research has resulted in over 870 publications and focuses on light-driven processes in molecules and materials, artificial photosynthesis, molecular electronics, quantum information science, ultrafast optical spectroscopy, and time-resolved electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy. He is member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and is the recipient of numerous awards.
