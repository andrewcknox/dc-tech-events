### Dreams of Scholarship: 150 Years of Discovery
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/03/22/dreams-of-scholarship-150-years-of-discovery/
Published: 
When: March 22nd 10:00am - February 21st 5:00pm
Where: George Peabody Library, Peabody Institute
Founded on a bold vision of deep study and imaginative thinking, Johns Hopkins University has long been a place where dreams of scholarship spark new ideas and the tenacity to test them. Drawing on rare books, photographs, archival documents, scientific findings, artifacts, and digital collections,

### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### The HOP Lecture Series Presents: A Conversation with Elias Zerhouni

While at the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine, Zerhouni also served as vice dean for research and as executive vice dean. Zerhouni is recognized for pioneering contributions to the field of radiology and public service as director of the NIH and as global science envoy and adviser, fostering collaborative leadership and solutions.

### The Bitter Struggle: Superintelligence, Superpowers, and the Fate of the World
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/11/the-bitter-struggle-superintelligence-superpowers-and-the-fate-of-the-world/
Published: 
When: November 11th 10:30am - 11:30am
Where: Hopkins Bloomberg Center
Join 
Ben Buchanan <https://sais.jhu.edu/directory/ben-buchanan>
, School of Advanced International Studies assistant professor and former White House special adviser for artificial intelligence, for a conversation on AI and national security with David E. Sanger, White House and national security correspondent for 
The New York Times
. The two will discuss Buchanan's new book 
The Bitter Struggle: Superintelligence, Superpowers, and the Fate of the World <https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/821154/the-bitter-struggle-by-ben-buchanan-and-tantum-collins/>
.
AI is becoming the decisive strategic technology of this century. Built by the private sector, it has profound implications for governments—and for all of us. Can the U.S. keep China from catching up in AI development, or do export controls just buy time? What happens when a handful of AI labs hold capabilities no government can match? And what if the hardest challenge is not between democracies and autocracies but at home, in the fight to make sure AI lives up to our democratic values?
