### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### PLAY: Scale Simulation-Based Learning with AI
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/10/play-scale-simulation-based-learning-with-ai/
Published: 
When: November 10th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
This workshop is part of the PLAY: Pedagogy, Learning, and AI in Your Classroom series from the Bloomberg School of Public Health's 
Center for Teaching and Learning <https://publichealth.jhu.edu/offices-and-services/center-for-teaching-and-learning>
.
Simulation-based learning helps students apply knowledge, make decisions, and practice navigating real-world challenges. This PLAY workshop will explore the benefits and pedagogy behind effective simulations, followed by a case study with 
Davina Frick <https://carey.jhu.edu/faculty/faculty-directory/k-davina-frick-phd>
, full-time faculty at Carey School of Business. She will share how she transformed one of her courses through simulation-based learning, including the instructional decisions, student experience, and lessons learned along the way. Frick will also explore how artificial intelligence (AI) supported the design process and discuss how AI can help make simulations more scalable and sustainable for future courses.
