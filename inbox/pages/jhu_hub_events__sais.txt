### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### Analyzing and Interpreting Your Research Data with AI
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/09/15/analysing-and-interpreting-your-research-data-with-ai-0/
Published: 
When: September 15th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Learn to make informed decisions about artificial intelligence (AI) use in your research by examining its role across data preparation, analyses, interpretation, and visualization.
There are two opportunities to attend this session:
Sept. 15, noon to 1 p.m.
Sept. 17, 9-10 a.m.
How can AI help me design or automate the data collection process?
How can I critically assess the outputs of AI-generated code?
How can I protect sensitive or unpublished data when using AI?
AI can support researchers at many stages of experimentation, from collecting data to developing models, recognizing patterns and troubleshooting problems. Used well, it can save time and open up new ways of working. However, reliable analysis still depends on sound research design, well-managed data and a clear analytical plan. AI cannot compensate for fundamental flaws in a study or turn poor-quality data into reliable evidence.
The challenge for researchers is knowing where AI adds genuine value and how to use it responsibly. How can they verify its outputs and manage risks such as bias, data privacy and security, and conclusions that go beyond the evidence?
In this webinar, you'll learn from distinguished panelists with expertise in AI research, editorial practice and policy. They will examine the role of AI across data preparation, analyses, interpretation and visualization. They will offer practical guidance and explain where human judgement and subject expertise remain essential. Whether you are new to AI or already using it in your work, the webinar will help you make more informed decisions about its role in your research.
You will have the opportunity to submit your own questions when you register. Please keep them relevant to analyzing and interpreting research data with assistance from AI. Covered topics will help you to:
Understand how AI can support different stages of data analysis, from planning and preparation to coding, troubleshooting and interpreting scientific results
Evaluate where AI adds value while recognizing when subject expertise, robust research methods and human oversight remain essential
Manage research data responsibly by validating AI outputs, protecting sensitive information, documenting AI use and following institutional, funder, and publisher guidance
Apply AI to strengthen, rather than replace, your own analytical thinking and research expertise
This session is hosted by Springer Nature Masterclasses for Johns Hopkins employees.

### LCSR Seminar Series: Keith Wear
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/09/16/lcsr-seminar-series-keith-wear-measurement-of-acoustic-pressure-with-hydrophones/
Published: 
When: September 16th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Keith Wear, recipient of the 2026 Distinguished Lecturer Award for Ultrasonics from the IEEE UFFC Society, presents "Measurement of Acoustic Pressure with Hydrophones" for the Laboratory for Computational Sensing + Robotics.
Accurate acoustic pressure measurements with hydrophones are critical to demonstrate safety and effectiveness of ultrasound devices as well as ensuring reproducibility of ultrasound experiments and treatments. Many ultrasound researchers perform hydrophone measurements, but few are aware of the distortions that hydrophones produce and the recently developed algorithms to correct for them. This tutorial will cover:
Physics of hydrophone designs (e.g., membrane, needle, capsule, reflection-type fiber optic, Fabry-Perot interferometric fiber optic)
Hydrophone specifications (e.g., sensitivity, directivity, effective sensitive element size)
Pros and cons of the various hydrophone designs for different applications
Practical advice for water-tank measurements
Deconvolution methods to remove pressure measurement distortions due to frequency-dependent sensitivity and spatial averaging artifacts.
For more about this event, visit the LCSR website <https://lcsr.jhu.edu/event/lcsr-seminar-series-keith-wear/>
.
