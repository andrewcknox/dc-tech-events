### Dreams of Scholarship: 150 Years of Discovery
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/03/22/dreams-of-scholarship-150-years-of-discovery/
Published: 
When: March 22nd 10:00am - February 21st 5:00pm
Where: George Peabody Library, Peabody Institute
Founded on a bold vision of deep study and imaginative thinking, Johns Hopkins University has long been a place where dreams of scholarship spark new ideas and the tenacity to test them. Drawing on rare books, photographs, archival documents, scientific findings, artifacts, and digital collections, 
this exhibition <https://www.library.jhu.edu/dreams-of-scholarship/>

It also highlights the vital contributions of the faculty, students, staff, and supporters whose work has expanded access to knowledge and shaped the future of inquiry at Johns Hopkins.

### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### LIQUID IDENTITY: Sandy Stone &amp; Rahne Alexander in Conversation
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/13/liquid-identity-sandy-stone-rahne-alexander-in-conversation/
Published: 
When: November 13th 6:00pm - 8:00pm
Where: Gilman Hall, Homewood Campus
Join 
Johns Hopkins' Common Question <https://studentaffairs.jhu.edu/common-question/>
 for LIQUID IDENTITY, a lively conversation with Sandy Stone and Rahne Alexander. Jumping off from Stone's revolutionary "The 
Empire
 Strikes Back: A Posttranssexual Manifesto" into her wild ride through multiple life paths, they'll talk about rock stars' secret habits, pirate history, viral academics, and how to speak the unspeakable.
The doors open at 6 p.m. and the talk begins at 6:30 p.m.; reception to follow.
Allucquére Rosanne "Sandy" Stone <https://sandystone.com/>
 is professor emerita and founding director of the Advanced Communication Technologies Laboratory (ACTLab) and the Convergent Media program at the University of Texas at Austin. As a recording engineer, she worked with Jimi Hendrix and engineered more than 100 rock, folk, blues, and classical albums. She is the first trans woman inducted into the National Women's Hall of Fame, and her archives will be housed at Harvard University's Radcliffe Schlesinger Library for the History of Women in America. Considered the founder of transgender studies and a pioneer in new media, she is also an author and multimedia performance artist. She stars in 
Girl Island: The Sandy Stone Story
, a hybrid feature documentary currently in post-production.
Rahne Alexander <https://msac.org/directory/artists/rahne-alexander>
 is a creative polymath—intermedia artist, musician, writer, event producer—and senior publicist for Hopkins Press Journals. Formerly the frontwoman of The Degenerettes and Santa Librada, she now kicks out her trans/queer jams in 50′♀ & the Worms. A fixture of the Baltimore LGBTQ and arts scenes, Alexander is a Baker Artist Award winner, a periodical contributor to publications like 
BmoreArt
 and 
UMBC Magazine
, and a sought-after conversation partner for artists and authors at Red Emma's, Greedy Reads, Atomic Books, and more.

### AstraZeneca National Healthcare Case Competition

Published:

Where: Hopkins Bloomberg Center
Showcase your talent, build your network, and develop critical business skills. This premier graduate-level national competition presents a unique opportunity to analyze a real-world 
healthcare business challenge <https://carey.jhu.edu/events/astrazeneca-national-healthcare-case-competition>
