### Dreams of Scholarship: 150 Years of Discovery
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/03/22/dreams-of-scholarship-150-years-of-discovery/
Published: 
When: March 22nd 10:00am - February 21st 5:00pm
Where: George Peabody Library, Peabody Institute
Founded on a bold vision of deep study and imaginative thinking, Johns Hopkins University has long been a place where dreams of scholarship spark new ideas and the tenacity to test them. Drawing on rare books, photographs, archival documents, scientific findings, artifacts, and digital collections, 
this exhibition <https://www.library.jhu.edu/dreams-of-scholarship/>
 traces the evolution of the university's libraries and their enduring impact on teaching, research, and student discovery. It also highlights the vital contributions of the faculty, students, staff, and supporters whose work has expanded access to knowledge and shaped the future of inquiry at Johns Hopkins.
Plan your visit <https://www.library.jhu.edu/library-hours/george-peabody-library/>
 to the George Peabody Library's Exhibit Gallery.

### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### Protecting Human Subject Data Privacy Part II: De-identification Techniques
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/06/protecting-human-subject-data-privacy-part-ii-de-identification-techniques/
Published: 
When: November 6th 12:00pm - 1:30pm
Prerequisite: Protecting Human Subject Data Privacy: An Introduction or reviewing our online resources 
This class builds upon the introductory session on privacy protection for shared data as a next step toward applying techniques for de-identifying several types of personal and health identifiers. Join 
Johns Hopkins Data Services <https://dataservices.library.jhu.edu/>
 to work through case examples and anonymization methods at an "intermediate" level suitable for preparing data for external collaborations and restricted access research databases. Presenters also discuss the more common advanced statistical techniques for anonymization and risk assessment and their use cases as well as current software options.
Note: If you attended Data Services' de-identification webinar in prior years, reviewing the 
online module and guide <https://dataservices.library.jhu.edu/resources/#deidentify>
 or webinar recording can prepare you for this session.

### Webinar | ChatGPT, We Need to Talk: AI &amp; Your Money
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/09/chatgpt-we-need-to-talk-ai-your-money-webinar/
Published: 
When: November 9th 5:00pm - 6:00pm
Artificial intelligence might be able to write your email. But should it write your financial plan?
In "ChatGPT, We Need to Talk: AI & Your Money," part of 
Student Financial Support <https://sfs.jhu.edu/>
's Financial Wellness webinar series, discover how AI can help you budget, plan, and make more informed financial decisions—while learning where to draw the line between helpful technology and advice you definitely shouldn't take at face value. The Financial Wellness Webinar Series is hosted by personal finance expert Shahar Ziv.
This webinar will cover:
How AI can help with budgeting, saving, and everyday financial decisions
Using AI to set financial goals and create personalized money strategies
What AI can—and can't—tell you about investing and other financial topics
How to spot inaccurate, biased, or misleading financial information generated by AI
Privacy and security: what you should never share with an AI tool
Tips for using AI as a financial resource without letting it make the decisions for you
Three students who attend the session will win a budget planner and Sony wireless on-ear headphones. Students who attend 
at least six financial wellness webinars during the year <https://sfs.jhu.edu/events/>
 will be entered to win an iPad.
