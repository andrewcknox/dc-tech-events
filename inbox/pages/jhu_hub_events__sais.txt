### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### LCSR Seminar Series: Jaime Fernández Fisac
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/11/lcsr-seminar-series-jaime-fernndez-fisac-neural-safety-filters-scaling-safety-critical-control-without-surrendering-guarantees/
Published: 
When: November 11th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Jaime Fernández Fisac, an assistant professor of electrical and computer engineering at Princeton University, presents "Neural Safety Filters: Scaling Safety-Critical Control Without Surrendering Guarantees" for the Laboratory for Computational Sensing + Robotics.
The expanding reach of robotics and artificial intelligence brings acute reliability challenges in the face of limited information, rapidly changing conditions, and close human interaction. Recent years have seen a surge of interest in safety filters, runtime schemes that monitor a system's candidate actions and intervene to preempt catastrophic failures. Yet the restrictive assumptions of model-based approaches and the weakened guarantees of data-driven alternatives have reinforced the perception of a fundamental safety—performance tradeoff.
This talk presents theoretical and experimental evidence against the view that strong safety guarantees must come at the expense of performance. Building on a unified understanding of safety filter mechanics, it introduces a general class of neural safety filters that extend the rigorous guarantees of safety-critical control to regimes beyond the reach of classical synthesis.
For more about this event, visit the LCSR website <https://lcsr.jhu.edu/event/lcsr-seminar-series-jaime_fernandez_fisac/>
.

### DMSE Fall 2026 Seminar Series | From Superwood to Artificial Sun: Inventing Materials through Scalable Innovation
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/11/dmse-fall-2026-seminar-series-from-superwood-to-artificial-sun-inventing-materials-through-scalable-innovation/
Published: 
When: November 11th 3:00pm - 4:00pm
Where: Maryland Hall, Homewood Campus
Liangbing Hu <https://engineering.yale.edu/research-and-faculty/faculty-directory/liangbing-hu>
, a professor of electrical and computer engineering and materials science at Yale University, will give a talk titled "From Superwood to Artificial Sun: Inventing Materials through Scalable Innovation" 
Department of Materials Science & Engineering <https://engineering.jhu.edu/materials/>
.
Abstract
Hu's group focuses on inventing new classes of materials through disruptive yet scalable processes, with the goal of enabling practical applications. In this talk, Hu will highlight several recent material innovations that open pathways toward emerging technologies:
Superwood: Nanostructure-engineered wood for next-generation sustainable buildings (
Nature
, 2018).
Molecular-scale wood nanofibers: Engineered fibers as solid-state battery electrolytes (
Nature
, 2021).
High-entropy catalysts: Designed via shock synthesis for energy and chemical transformations (
Science
, 2018).
Ultrafast high-temperature sintering: Rapid processing of oxide-based ion conductors for batteries and solid oxide electrolysis cells (
Science
, 2020).
Ultrahigh-temperature stable plasma (also known as "artificial sun"): A platform for extreme materials synthesis (
Nature
, 2023).
Dynamic heating strategies: Conversion of waste plastics into jet fuel, and methane pyrolysis for high value products (
Nature
, 2022; 
Nature Chemical Engineering
, 2025).
Together, these advances illustrate how nanoscale design, extreme processing conditions, and unconventional approaches can converge to create disruptive materials solutions for energy, sustainability, and advanced manufacturing.
Bio
Liangbing Hu also serves as the founding director of the Yale Center for Materials Innovation. He joined Yale's faculty in July 2024 after serving as a distinguished university professor at the University of Maryland, College Park. Hu's research group specializes in materials innovation, device integration, and advanced manufacturing, with current projects in electrified ultrahigh-temperature synthesis, next-generation energy storage beyond lithium-ion, and novel wood nanotechnologies.
He has authored approximately 500 peer-reviewed publications, including more than 10 articles in 
Science
 and 
Nature
, with an h-index of 200. His work has been recognized with several prestigious awards, including the American Chemical Society's (ACS) Anselme Payen Award, the Materials Research Society (MRS) Medal, five R&D 100 Awards, and the Nature Spinoff Prize. He is an MRS and ACS fellow.
Hu earned a Bachelor of Science in physics from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2002 and a doctorate in physics from the University of California, Los Angeles in 2007. Based on his doctoral research on carbon nanotube thin films, he became a founding scientist at Unidym, a Silicon Valley startup specializing in printed electronics. From 2009 to 2011, Hu completed a postdoctoral fellowship at Stanford University. In 2011, he joined the University of Maryland, where he launched his independent academic career.
