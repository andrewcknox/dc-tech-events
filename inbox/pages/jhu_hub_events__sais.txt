### If Homewood's Walls Could Talk: A History of an American House
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/05/13/if-homewoods-walls-could-talk-a-history-of-an-american-house/
Published: 
When: May 13th 11:00am - January 11th 4:00pm
Where: Homewood Museum, Homewood Campus
In conjunction with the 250th anniversary of the U.S. and 
the 150th anniversary of Johns Hopkins University <https://150.jhu.edu/>
, this housewide exhibition explores Homewood's 224-year history, from its 1801 construction for the family of Declaration of Independence signer and enslaver Charles Carroll of Carrollton, through its 1902 acquisition by Johns Hopkins University, to its 1980s restoration and launch as a historic house museum. Using archival photography, textiles, student diaries, historic documents, furniture, oral histories, and more, the exhibition amplifies the voices of those who lived, worked, or learned at Homewood, allowing visitors to understand how individual histories contribute to a larger story of the house, the university, and the U.S.
The exhibition, available Tuesday–Sunday, 11 a.m.–4 p.m., is on view through Jan. 10, 2027.

### LCSR Seminar Series: Defining the Discipline of Robotics for Excellence and Equity in an AI-Driven World
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/04/lcsr-seminar-series-odest-chadwicke-jenkins-defining-the-discipline-of-robotics-for-excellence-and-equity-in-an-ai-driven-world/
Published: 
When: November 4th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Where: Hackerman Hall, Homewood Campus
Odest Chadwicke "Chad" Jenkins <https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~ocj/>
, a professor of robotics and of electrical engineering and computer science at the University of Michigan, will give a talk titled "Defining the Discipline of Robotics for Excellence and Equity in an AI-driven World" for the 
Laboratory for Computational Sensing + Robotics <https://lcsr.jhu.edu/>
.
Abstract:
Start with a simple question: What is the best major for a student to become a roboticist? In my role leading the creation of the robotics undergraduate major at Michigan, we tackled this question through the curricular challenge of how to both: 1) educate people to put ideas of the robotics discipline into practice and 2) endow them with the intellectual lens for creating new ideas that extend the frontiers of the robotics discipline—where mobility and dexterity through humanoid robots are poised to have a transformative impact in our increasingly AI-driven world.
In this talk, I will discuss my work to both advance the state of the art in semantic understanding for robotics and prepare students for possible futures where robotics and AI are abundant and ubiquitous. I will present the design, launch, and innovations for the Michigan robotics major for undergraduates, as robotics emerges into its own as a true academic discipline. I will describe highlights from research in semantic 3D perception for mobile manipulation robots (including humanoids) to understand the form and function (or affordances) for a diversity of objects in common environments. I will also share insights from my role as program chair of the AAAI-26 Conference (and its roughly 30,000 submissions) and how this role, and my experiences with Amazon AWS Machine Learning University, has reshaped my perspective on AI for teaching at the university level. Finally, I will discuss new efforts in my course this semester to formulate "mutation assessments" that gauge student understanding of agentically coded projects.
Bio:
Jenkins' research addresses problems in robot learning from demonstration and human-robot interaction, primarily focused on dexterous mobile manipulation and robot perception. He was the founding chair of the robotics major degree program launched in 2022 for undergraduates at the University of Michigan. Jenkins was recently program chair for the AAAI-26 Conference and inaugural editor-in-chief for the 
ACM Transactions on Human-Robot Interaction
. Jenkins earned his Bachelor of Science in computer science and mathematics at Alma College (1996), Master of Science in computer science at Georgia Tech (1998), and doctorate in computer science at the University of Southern California (2003). Jenkins is a fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science, Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence, and the Association for Computing Machinery.

### What Are Students Learning? A Practical Workshop on Assessment Review and Redesign (Online)
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/04/what-are-students-learning-a-practical-workshop-on-assessment-review-and-redesign/
Published: 
When: November 4th 1:00pm - 2:00pm
Olysha Magruder, assistant dean of teaching and learning design, and Amir Saeed, adjunct faculty in data science and artificial intelligence at the Whiting School of Engineering, Data Science, will present "What Are Students Learning? A Practical Workshop on Assessment Review and Redesign."
This hands-on workshop helps faculty examine how their assessments support learning and provide evidence of what students know and can do in the age of artificial intelligence. Using a guided rubric, participants will analyze an engineering assessment, critique redesign recommendations from the Course AI Auditor, and apply the same process to an assessment from their own teaching. ### DMSE Fall 2026 Seminar Series | NIST and Material Measurement: Enabling Trustworthy, High-Value Tools for Science-Based Businesses in the Age of AI
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/04/dmse-fall-2026-seminar-series-nist-and-material-measurement-enabling-trustworthy-high-value-tools-for-science-based-businesses-in-the-age-of-ai/
Published: 
When: November 4th 3:00pm - 4:00pm
Where: Maryland Hall, Homewood Campus
Kate Beers <https://www.nist.gov/people/kathryn-l-beers>
, the director of the Material Measurement Laboratory (MML) at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), will discuss enabling trustworthy, high-value tools for science-based businesses in the age of artificial intelligence for the 
Department of Materials Science & Engineering <https://engineering.jhu.edu/materials/>
.
As part of the U.S. National Metrology Institute, the NIST laboratories play a key role in advancing trust in measurement and technology in ways that enhance U.S. economic security and quality of life. For NIST's MML, impacts focus on measurement innovation, measurement services, and the convening power of an objective, nonregulatory agency dedicated to industrial translation in materials science, chemistry, and biology. Examples in this talk will illustrate these areas of impact and then dive deeper into work MML researchers are conducting to improve the quality of data and analytical tools at the interface of traditional scientific research and the new world of discovery enabled by AI and machine learning technologies. Examples will include orthogonal training datasets for improved interpretation of near-infrared spectroscopy, autonomous sampling of complex compositional spaces, and the next generation of reference materials and reference data products.
Speaker bio
Kathryn "Kate" Beers is the director of the MML at the NIST in the U.S. Department of Commerce. MML, one of NIST's six research laboratories, conducts measurement science research in the chemical, biological, and material sciences and contributes technical expertise to the development of standards through MML's Office of Reference Materials. MML also stewards a large portfolio of data products, including standard reference data, and world-class programs supporting food safety, forensic science, illicit drug detection, semiconductors, and emerging biotechnology, as well as many critical manufacturing sectors.
Beers has served as a bench scientist and project leader, directed the Combinatorial Methods Center, and led staff and programs as Group Leader and Deputy Division Chief. In her most recent role, prior to becoming MML Director, she served as the Circular Economy Program Director. She has more than 100 publications spanning many areas of soft matter and polymer science.
In addition, Beers has held influential roles externally such as assistant director for physical sciences and engineering at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, chair of the Polymer Chemistry Division of the American Chemical Society (ACS), and chair of the scientific advisory board of the ACS Green Chemistry Institute. She has served on many interagency working groups and on editorial and advisory boards. She has been recognized with numerous awards, including the Arthur S. Flemming Award for Public Service, the Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers, the Department of Commerce Silver Medal, and the Mark Scholar Award from the ACS Division of Polymer Chemistry. She is an ACS Fellow. Beers earned a Master of Science in polymer science and a doctorate in chemistry from Carnegie Mellon University and a Bachelor of Science in chemistry with high honors from The College of William and Mary.

### Faculty Voice, Learner Success: Humanizing Quality in Course Design
Link: https://hub.jhu.edu/events/2026/11/05/faculty-voice-learner-success-humanizing-quality-in-course-design/
Published: 
When: November 5th 12:00pm - 1:00pm
Join Madalina Tincu, senior instructional designer with the 
Johns Hopkins Advanced Academic Programs Instructional Research Center <https://irc.jhu.edu/index.html>
, to explore how human-centered learning objectives can make student thinking visible, support meaningful engagement with artificial intelligence, and lay the foundation for authentic assessment.
As AI reshapes how students access, generate, and apply information, faculty are increasingly asking: What should students know, do, and become in an AI-enabled world? This interactive micro-workshop explores how to humanize Bloom's Taxonomy by moving beyond traditional cognitive verbs and redesigning learning objectives that cultivate critical thinking, ethical reasoning, creativity, reflection, and authentic application.
Through guided discussion and hands-on redesign of activities, participants will leave with practical strategies for creating rigorous learning experiences that emphasize process, judgment, and real-world application in an increasingly AI-rich educational landscape.
